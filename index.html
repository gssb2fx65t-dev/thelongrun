The road ahead
2027 → Dopey 2028
The 2026 schedule (through Feb 2027) is built. This is the shape of what comes next: a base phase, a marathon in October 2027, and the Dopey Challenge in January 2028. Details to be designed closer to the time — this is the agreed framework.
Mar 1 – mid-Jun 2027
Base Phase
~25–30 mi/wk
You arrive March 1 carrying the fall base (preserved through the ski winter): ~22 mi/wk, long run 10. This phase is a gentle on-ramp — hold and lightly lift toward ~30 mi/wk with a long run around 12–13, re-establishing 5-day running. No heroics; the job is to be ready to absorb the marathon build, not to peak. Roughly 15 weeks.
April 1, 2027
Dopey go / no-go
Decision
By this point the entire offseason and all of March are done — a full month into the base phase. This is the go/no-go gate: based on where your training actually stands (base intact, long run progressing, body healthy), commit to the Dopey Challenge and register, or hold for another year. If it’s a go, this is when you sign up.
mid-Jun – mid-Oct 2027
Marathon Build (18 wk)
peak ~40–45
An 18-week build to the marathon (~mid-Oct 2027). Starts from the ~25–30 mi base and climbs to a peak of 40–45 mi/wk — a peak, not a floor — with long runs building to the classic 20–22. Gentle by design, in the spirit of the Higdon approach: durability and consistency over raw volume. 5 days/week with the Priority-5 drop day.
mid-Oct 2027
Marathon
26.2
The goal race of 2027 and the first marathon of the comeback. Everything in the base and build phases points here. Recover deliberately afterward — the marathon fitness is the foundation for Dopey, which follows just ~12 weeks later.
mid-Oct 2027 – early Jan 2028
Marathon → Dopey Bridge
~12 wk
Too short for a separate build — the marathon fitness carries. Recover (~2–3 weeks), then sharpen consecutive-day tolerance for ~8–9 weeks: the back-to-back weekend work rehearsed in fall 2026 scales up here. The marathon build IS Dopey’s aerobic foundation; this phase just adds the four-days-in-a-row specificity.
~Jan 6–9, 2028
Dopey Challenge
48.6 mi / 4 days
5K (Thu) + 10K (Fri) + Half (Sat) + Full (Sun) over four consecutive days at Walt Disney World. Modeled on the Higdon Dopey plan: 18 weeks, opens at a 13-mile long run, peaks at only ~20 mi long / ~38 mi week — gentle mileage, but built entirely around the consecutive-day pattern.
When ready
Design the 2027 plan

When it’s time to build the detailed 2027 schedule, here is the agreed framework to start from.

Structure: 5 days/week, Priority-5 drop day. Hierarchy: Sat long (P1), Tue anchor (P2), Wed/Thu medium (P3), Sun recovery (P4), Mon/Fri easy (P5 — drop). Drop day never adjacent to the long run.

Volume: base phase ~25–30; marathon build peaks 40–45 (a peak, not a floor). Gentle, Higdon-style.

Shape: base (Mar–Jun) → 18-wk marathon build → marathon (Oct) → recover + consecutive-day sharpening → Dopey (Jan).

2026 data confirmed: Tue is the most reliable day (P2); Mon the least used (ideal P5 drop). Back-to-back weekend tolerance already being built in fall 2026.
Guide ✕
Sleep
Sleep is the highest-leverage thing
Your body does not get fitter during runs - it adapts during recovery, and that process is almost entirely sleep-dependent. During heavy training weeks (20+ miles) you likely need 30-60 min more than usual.
Two nights before matters most
The night before Saturday long run matters less than Wednesday and Thursday nights. One bad Friday does not derail a long run nearly as much as poor mid-week sleep.
Alcohol and sleep quality
Alcohol disrupts sleep depth even when it does not reduce hours. You get the hours but not the restorative depth. Particularly relevant the night before any hard effort.
Nutrition
Protein timing
The 30-60 min window after Saturday long run is when muscles are most receptive to protein. Aim for 25-40g in that window. During heavier training weeks, target 100-130g protein per day total.
Do not run fasted on long runs
As Saturday distances grow past 7-8 miles, running without breakfast compromises quality and recovery. A simple breakfast 60-90 minutes before is enough. Do not skip this.
Carbs are your fuel
During weeks above 15 miles, carbohydrates are your primary fuel. Restricting them makes runs harder, slows recovery, and raises injury risk. The grain pouches in meal prep are doing important work.
Eat enough
Appetite suppresses after long runs so under-fueling is common. On days with a long run you need meaningfully more food than rest days.
Dinner priority — normal weeks (Tue/Wed/Sat/Sun runs)
Ranked 1 (critical) to 7 (cheat day):
1. Friday — night before the long run. White rice or pasta, lean protein, low fat/fiber, by 7pm. Zero flexibility.
2. Thursday — start of the 36-hour window. Same clean approach.
3. Saturday — recovery: carbs + protein for Sunday's run. Nourishing, not strict.
4. Monday — fuels Tuesday's anchor run. Sensible.
5. Tuesday — low stakes, Wednesday is short.
6. Wednesday — outer edge of the runway. Avoid a true blowout.
7. Sunday — the cheat day. Both runs done, longest runway. Once recovery boxes are checked, dinner is yours. Only rule: do not wreck sleep.
Dinner priority — swap weeks (Fri run, Sun long run)
When the short run moves to Friday and the long run to Sunday:
1. Saturday — now the night before the long run. Full pre-run protocol.
2. Friday — dual role: post-run recovery + start of the window. Clean carbs and lean protein.
3. Thursday — fuels Friday's run, edge of the long runway. Sensible and clean.
4. Sunday — recovery-first, treat-second. No run until Tuesday.
5. Monday — fuels Tuesday. Sensible.
6. Tuesday — low stakes.
7. Wednesday — the cheat day on swap weeks. Longest clean runway.

Condensed: normal weeks — Thu/Fri sacred, Sun free. Swap weeks — Fri/Sat sacred, Wed free.
Hydration & Fueling
When to start
Hydration awareness starts now. Mid-run fueling threshold: Week 14 (Saturday hits 8 miles) — start experimenting. Essential by Week 17 (Saturday at 9 miles, over 90 minutes). Heat block Week 21: shift to active pre-hydration starting Thursday evening.
Tailwind as an all-in-one
Handles hydration and fueling simultaneously. Mix 2 scoops in 20oz water, drink every 15-20 minutes from mile 1. Gentler on the stomach than most gels. One bottle covers a 90-minute effort.
The 36-hour protocol
Thursday evening: familiar dinner, lean protein, white rice or pasta, low fat, low fiber. Friday: carbohydrate-forward meals, light electrolyte drink afternoon, dinner by 7pm. Saturday breakfast 60-75 min before: plain bagel with peanut butter and banana, or oatmeal. 16-20oz water with breakfast. Nothing new in the entire 36-hour window.
Long run countdown — dinner to start line
Night before: Dinner by 7pm — white rice or pasta, lean protein, low fat, low fiber, no beans. 16-20oz water with dinner, 8oz before bed. Nothing new.
Wake-up (60-90 min out): 12-16oz water immediately. Coffee now if you have it.
Breakfast (60-75 min out): Plain bagel with thin peanut butter + banana, or oatmeal with honey. 8oz water. Keep fat thin, nothing new.
10-15 min out: 4-6oz water only — no more. Bathroom stop.
On the run (7+ miles, or comfort score under 8, or dew point over 55°F): Carry Tailwind — 2 scoops in 18-20oz. Sip from mile 1, every 15-20 minutes. Half the bottle gone by the halfway point.
Within 30 min of finishing: 20-25g protein (Recovery Mix, Greek yogurt, eggs) + keep drinking water.
